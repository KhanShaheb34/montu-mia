---
title: "C, A, and P - What Are They?"
---

Boltu Bhai grabbed a marker and wrote three big letters on the whiteboard: C, A, P.

— "Listen, Montu. You'll read on the internet or in textbooks that the CAP theorem means picking any two out of these three. But in the world of distributed systems, that is a flat-out lie."

Montu looked confused. "What do you mean, a lie? Bhai, you literally just said..."

"Listen, rookie, let me finish!" Boltu Bhai scolded playfully. "A distributed system, by definition, means you have multiple servers—just like those multiple bus counters. And when you have multiple servers, network cables _will_ get cut, routers _will_ fry, or water _will_ leak into a satellite. In other words, a **Partition (P)** is inevitable. You can't prevent it, no matter how hard you try."

![partition-tolerance](/images/sd/trade-offs/3.png)

Boltu Bhai underlined the 'P'.

— "Since there's no way to avoid P, you don't actually have three options—you only have two. When P happens, you have to sacrifice either C or A. Let's break these three down in plain English:"

- **P - Partition Tolerance:** Even if the cable between your two servers gets cut or communication drops, the system won't crash and burn. It will stay alive and kicking. In a distributed system, this is a 100% non-negotiable requirement.
- **A - Availability:** The system will always respond. No matter what happens to the servers, if a user makes a request, they will get _some_ kind of answer (even if it's outdated or slightly incorrect data). The system will never just sit there showing a 'Server Down' message to the user.
- **C - Consistency:** All servers in the system will always hold the exact same data. A user checking from Dhaka will see the exact same data as a user checking from New York. No one will ever see wrong or outdated information.

Montu nodded. "So that means, Bhai, for any distributed system, I have to choose either **CP** (Consistency + Partition Tolerance) or **AP** (Availability + Partition Tolerance)?"

"Bingo!" Boltu Bhai said happily. "Now let me give you two real-life examples. That'll show you exactly when to build a CP system and when to build an AP system."
