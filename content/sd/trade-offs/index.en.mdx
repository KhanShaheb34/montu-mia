title: "Can You Really Have It All?"
description: "Wait, what's a trade-off?"
---

Life had been sailing smoothly for Montu lately. BiralTube was blazing fast, and the constant dread of servers crashing was finally gone. But that nagging, perfectionist itch in his startup-founder brain just wouldn't leave him alone.

Sipping coffee late at night, Montu pondered, "I worked so hard to build this distributed system. I did the data partitioning, the replication, everything! So why isn't it perfect? If I push for speed, the data takes time to sync across all servers (Replication Lag). But if I force instant syncing, the whole system slows down. Isn't there some magic solution where it's rocket-fast, perfectly synced everywhere, _and_ has 100% uptime?"

Frustrated, Montu called Boltu Bhai the next morning. After hearing the whole rant, Boltu Bhai burst out laughing.

"Listen, rookie! You can't just have everything you want in this world," Boltu Bhai chuckled. "You've stepped into the realm of distributed systems. Out here, the name of the game is 'trade-offs', sacrificing one thing to get another. Come over to my place this afternoon. Let me show you the kind of disaster that strikes when you try to have it all."

Later that afternoon, Montu Mia rushed over and planted himself in Boltu Bhai's living room.

Boltu Bhai brought out two cups of tea, handed one to Montu, and immediately threw a curveball. "Alright Montu, tell me something. Ever since you launched BiralTube, have you been able to balance the app, taking care of yourself, and spending time with your girlfriend?"

Montu took a sip of his tea and sighed heavily.

— "Not at all, Bhai. If I focus on BiralTube, my dating life tanks. If I try to focus on my relationship, I barely get six hours of sleep. I'm just running on a treadmill all day. But... why are you asking me this out of nowhere?"

![trade-offs](/images/sd/trade-offs/1.png)

Boltu Bhai smirked.

— "There's a reason. What you're saying is, if you focus heavily on one thing, your time and energy for the other things drop. You constantly have to strike a balance, right?"

— "Yeah, Bhai, exactly."

— "That right there is a **trade-off**," Boltu Bhai explained. "To get better results in one area, you have to make sacrifices in another. It's the exact same story with distributed systems. If you want absolute perfection on one front, you have to tolerate a little pain on another. You can't just have your cake and eat it too, right?"

Boltu Bhai set his teacup down on the table, his tone turning serious. "Let's break down exactly what you can, and cannot, get when you design a system."
